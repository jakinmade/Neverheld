NEVERHELD locks a web page so it can't send what you type to anyone. Then it gives you a receipt you can check yourself, without having to trust the company.

Try it now: no sign-upHow it works
1

Lock

When the page has loaded, NEVERHELD tells your browser: "this page may not send anything anywhere." The browser enforces it.

2

Count

If anything on the page tries to send your data, such as a hidden ad tracker, the browser refuses, and NEVERHELD counts the attempt where you can see it.

3

Receipt

You download a receipt of what happened. You can check it on any computer, even offline. If someone changes it, the check fails.

Why this matters

Most websites carry hidden "trackers" from ad and analytics companies. On a normal shop that's annoying. On a page where you describe your health, your money or a legal problem, it's a real leak, and companies are paying for it in court.

Without NEVERHELD

You type "chest pain since Tuesday" into a hospital's form. A tracker on the page quietly sends it to an ad company. Nobody can prove afterwards what was or wasn't sent.

With NEVERHELD

The same form is locked. The tracker tries, the browser says no, and the counter shows it. The patient and the hospital both get a receipt of what the page did.

For companies: try it on your own page in 30 seconds

Nothing to download and nothing to install. Copy one line, paste it at the end of the page you want to lock, and reload. That's it.

<script src="https://neverheld-demo-production.up.railway.app/neverheld.min.js" data-auto-seal data-workers-network="none"></script>
  1. Copy the line above.
  2. Paste it just before </body> on any page with a form.
  3. Reload the page. You'll see the NEVERHELD counter in the top right, and a "Proof and checks" button.

Works with plain HTML and Next.js. Tested in Chrome, Firefox and Safari's engine. For live use you get the same single file (about 13 KB) to host yourself, so your page doesn't depend on us.

How sure is this?

We would rather be exact than oversell it.

  • Enforced by the browser: once locked, the page's normal ways of sending data (forms, uploads, background requests, trackers, images, scripts) are refused.
  • Also blocked by NEVERHELD itself: WebRTC and network "hints". This is done by the page, so determined code could get round it.
  • Not covered: browser extensions, other tabs, anything before the lock, opening a new window, a WebRTC route through hidden frames, and a Safari quirk with preconnect hints.
  • The receipt is written by the page itself. It is strong evidence, not a signed record or an independent audit. Three review rounds have been done and published; an independent audit has not.
